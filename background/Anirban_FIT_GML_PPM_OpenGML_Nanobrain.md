# Background: Anirban Bandyopadhyay, FIT, GML, PPM, OpenGML and Nanobrain

**Status:** Public background note  
**Author:** Martin Timms  
**Related project:** Adaptive Invariant Computation (AIC)  
**Date:** 30 September 2026

## Purpose

Adaptive Invariant Computation (AIC) has conceptual links to a body of work developed by **Dr Anirban Bandyopadhyay** and collaborators around information, nested geometry, phase, resonance, time structure and non-conventional models of computation.

Those connections matter to the history of the project, but AIC should **not** be described as a direct implementation of Fractal Information Theory (FIT), Geometric Musical Language (GML), Phase Prime Metric (PPM), OpenGML, or the physical artificial-brain programme described in *Nanobrain*.

AIC asks a narrower, independently testable engineering question:

> **Can task-relevant invariants provide an explicit control criterion for reducing computational representations while preserving useful structure?**

This note explains the relationship.

---

## 1. *Nanobrain* as the broad conceptual synthesis

Anirban Bandyopadhyay's book:

- **Anirban Bandyopadhyay, _Nanobrain: The Making of an Artificial Brain from a Time Crystal_**, CRC Press / Taylor & Francis, 2020.  
  https://www.routledge.com/Nanobrain-The-Making-of-an-Artificial-Brain-from-a-Time-Crystal/Bandyopadhyay/p/book/9781439875490

is the most useful single background source for understanding how FIT, GML and PPM sit together within Bandyopadhyay's wider programme.

The book explicitly develops:

- **Fractal Information Theory (FIT)** and the idea of a fractal information tape;
- **Geometric Musical Language (GML)** as a geometric/temporal representational language;
- **Phase Prime Metric (PPM)** as a phase- and prime-based geometric metric;
- nested time structures and clocking relationships;
- physical artificial-brain concepts based on resonant and time-crystal-like organisation.

For AIC, the value of *Nanobrain* is primarily conceptual. It repeatedly asks whether intelligence and information processing might be better understood through **nested geometry, phase relations and stable structural relationships** rather than only through sequential symbolic processing.

AIC does not adopt the book's full physical, biological or consciousness model. It extracts a much narrower computational question: **what structural relationships should remain stable as a representation is transformed or reduced?**

## 2. Fractal Information Theory (FIT)

A key academic reference is:

- **L. Agrawal, R. Chhajed, S. Ghosh, B. Ghosh, K. Ray, S. Sahu, D. Fujita and A. Bandyopadhyay, _Fractal Information Theory (FIT)-Derived Geometric Musical Language (GML) for Brain-Inspired Hypercomputing_.**  
  NIMS record: https://samurai.nims.go.jp/books/f05bf739-a383-4606-b998-d320c8e8309b?locale=en  
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-10-5699-4_33

FIT proposes a broader information-processing framework in which information is associated with nested temporal and geometric structures rather than only with a single linear sequence.

The idea of a **fractal tape** is important conceptually because it invites information to be represented across nested scales.

### Difference from AIC

FIT proposes a broad alternative information architecture.

AIC does not require a replacement for Shannon information theory, a new physical substrate, or a particular model of time. It can be implemented and tested using conventional software and conventional learned representations.

AIC asks whether an ordinary computational representation contains **task-relevant invariants** that can be measured and used to control reduction.

## 3. Geometric Musical Language (GML)

GML is the geometric language associated with FIT. It represents informational structure through relationships among geometry, timing, phase and nested organisation.

Relevant public sources include:

- the FIT/GML paper above;
- *Nanobrain*, especially its chapters on FIT and GML;
- the patent publication **Universal Geometric-Musical Language for big data processing in an assembly of clocking resonators**:  
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US20200160174A1/en  
  PCT publication: https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2019026983A1/en

GML is relevant to AIC because it encourages a shift away from treating a representation as merely an arbitrary vector or token sequence. It asks whether **geometry and relations carry structure that should survive transformations**.

### Difference from AIC

GML concerns a language and representational framework.

AIC is a proposed **adaptive computation principle**. It does not prescribe one geometric alphabet. It asks:

1. Which properties of a representation are important for the task?
2. Which of those properties should remain invariant under allowed transformations?
3. Can their preservation determine whether further computational reduction is safe?

AIC could in principle operate on GML-like structures, but it does not depend on GML.

## 4. Phase Prime Metric (PPM)

Bandyopadhyay's **Phase Prime Metric (PPM)** develops geometric relationships among integers and primes with emphasis on phase, symmetry, clockwise/anticlockwise paths and ordered factor structure.

A substantial treatment appears in Chapter 3 of *Nanobrain*:

- https://www.routledge.com/Nanobrain-The-Making-of-an-Artificial-Brain-from-a-Time-Crystal/Bandyopadhyay/p/book/9781439875490

PPM matters to the lineage of AIC because it illustrates a broader idea:

> **Structural relations can become visible when an object is represented geometrically and relationally rather than only symbolically.**

In experimental work around OpenGML and later invariant-space systems, PPM also motivated investigations of phase, mirrored structure, symmetry and chirality.

### Difference from AIC

AIC does not claim that prime-number geometry or PPM is a universal representation for intelligence.

PPM is better treated here as a **source of geometric intuition and an experimental representational substrate**.

AIC is more general: whatever representation is used, can its task-relevant invariants be identified and used to control computational reduction?

## 5. OpenGML

**OpenGML** is an open-source project by Martin Timms created to explore GML-inspired nested geometry, phase relationships, PPM visualisation, symmetry and related computational structures.

Repository:

- https://github.com/Electro-resonance/OpenGML

The repository contains 2D and 3D geometric frameworks, PPM visualisations, phase-related experiments, symmetry-breaking examples and sonification work.

A useful data-science-oriented overview is:

- https://github.com/Electro-resonance/OpenGML/blob/main/PPM%2C%20GML%2C%20and%20OpenGML%20for%20Data%20Scientist.md

OpenGML is important to the AIC lineage because it provided a software environment in which geometric and phase-oriented ideas could be inspected and manipulated computationally rather than remaining purely conceptual.

### Difference from AIC

- **OpenGML** is primarily a geometric representation and experimentation framework.
- **AIC** is a proposed adaptive computation mechanism.

AIC may draw intuition from OpenGML, but AIC is not a renamed OpenGML architecture.

## 6. From FIT/GML/PPM to invariant-space research

The common thread linking FIT, GML, PPM and OpenGML is not a single equation.

It is the idea that information can contain **persistent relational structure across transformations and scales**, and that geometry or phase may expose such structure more clearly than surface symbols alone.

That naturally suggests the language of **invariants**.

An invariant is a property that remains stable under a defined family of transformations.

Depending on the task, an invariant might be:

- a semantic relation;
- a graph topology;
- an ordering relation;
- a symmetry class;
- a conserved relational structure;
- a phase relationship;
- a geometric property;
- or a learned latent feature that remains predictive under task-preserving transformation.

This lineage contributed to later invariant-space work:

- https://github.com/Electro-resonance/Invariant-Space-LLM

That project asks whether a transformer can form a persistent internal workspace that seeks stable relational invariants beneath changing linguistic surface form.

AIC takes another step: **can preservation of those invariants regulate how much representation or computation should be retained?**

## 7. Why AIC is different

AIC is not intended to reproduce Bandyopadhyay's artificial-brain architecture in conventional software.

The distinctions are important:

| Research strand | Primary concern | Relationship to AIC |
|---|---|---|
| **FIT** | Nested/fractal information organisation | Motivates thinking across scales and nested representations |
| **GML** | Geometric and temporal language for information | Motivates relational/geometric representation |
| **PPM** | Prime/phase geometry and symmetry | Provides geometric and phase-oriented intuition |
| **Nanobrain** | Broad physical/artificial-brain synthesis of these ideas | Provides the wider conceptual context |
| **OpenGML** | Computational exploration of GML/PPM-inspired geometry | Provides an experimental software bridge |
| **Invariant-Space-LLM** | Persistent invariant-oriented representation in transformers | Direct precursor to invariant-first AI |
| **AIC** | Adaptive reduction governed by preservation of task-relevant invariants | New testable computation hypothesis |

The proposed novelty of AIC is therefore not simply "use geometry" or "use invariants."

Its research question is whether **invariant preservation can become an active control variable for computational reduction**.

That question can be tested using ordinary machine-learning baselines, controlled transformations, ablations and negative controls.

## 8. Phase preservation and information loss

Another useful connection concerns phase.

Bandyopadhyay's work places unusual emphasis on phase relationships and nested clocks. Whether or not one accepts the wider physical interpretation, this highlights a real computational issue: some transformations may preserve gross content while destroying **relationships among components**.

This is relevant to AI compression.

A representation can preserve individual features but alter:

- ordering;
- relative geometry;
- phase-like relations;
- causal dependence;
- topology;
- temporal relation.

AIC therefore treats "what survives compression?" as a richer question than simple reconstruction error.

The relevant invariant depends on the task.

In one system it may be semantic ordering. In another it may be a geometric relationship. In another it may be temporal or phase structure.

---

## 9. What AIC does not claim

AIC does **not** require the following claims to be accepted:

- that the brain is literally a time crystal;
- that PPM is a universal computational metric;
- that GML replaces conventional software;
- that FIT replaces standard information theory;
- that consciousness depends on the mechanisms proposed in *Nanobrain*.

Those are separate scientific questions.

AIC can fail or succeed experimentally without resolving them.

This separation is deliberate. It allows concepts inspired by unconventional research to be translated into a conventional, falsifiable AI experiment.
